---
title:  Our Second Attempt at a 24 Hour Hackathon
date: 2026-02-15
---

Lo and behold it's already been a year since we had participated in Kanam '25. The second edition of the hackathon was back - and so were we, fresh after our semester of intenships. With new found confidence and industry knowledge we stepped into a turf with new faces, new event management team and with a fresh batch of ideas.

We started off the hackathon with a slight loss on the account of Mithun not being able to make because he was feeling under the weather. Regardless, we braced ourselves and confidently entered the arena, equipped with Red Bulls. The problem statement was handed to us this time - instead of getting to choose our own ideas. This was a minor setback because we were asked to build a mobile app and me being the only person in the team with frontend experience, I had no formal experience in developing mobile applications save for a little bit of experimenting with Expo back in 2023.

It wasn't long enough before we pivotted to a PWA that will allow us to play it safe while still simulating the look and feel of a native app. For the next 24 hours, we iterated and re-iterated over the problem statement - a healthcare/vaccination app for the general public that will make it easier for people to get vaccinated, and for healthcare workers to maintain digital records of vaccinations. Sounds straightforward on paper, but the sheer amount of features we added to the app (thanks to Claude Code) resulted in a polished, almost shippable application that used real data for healthcare centers and vaccines.

The judge who has initially not too impressed with our feature set became enthralled not even 12 hours later due to us going on multiple sprints in that time. 

Coming back to the liveliness of the hackathon, it was certainly a downplay of last year. Teams were not allowed to socialize or speak because of possible cheating - which was completely understandable, but also diluted the energy of a hackathon where people socialize and use it as a place for networking - something we were able to do last year. What was a little more disappointing was the fact that none of the volunteers from the hosting institution seemed to be interested in what the teams were doing.

We managed to bag the first prize this time - partly because of the featureset and the rest because of how we pitched the application. Overall, Kanam '26 was not what we had prepared for, in multiple aspects, but ultimately gave us a chance at redemption from last year. I only wish we were able to see the same spark in a few people's eyes before leaving - and maybe find inspiration in someone else's work; this felt more like a competition for the prizes than a healthy exchange of knowledge. Regardless, it was a captivating experience, and since this was the last edition that we could've been part of, we did the best we could. ;p
